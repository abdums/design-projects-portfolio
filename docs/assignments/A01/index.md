# A1 – [Create Portfolio]

## Objective
The objective of this assignment is to create a professional engineering portfolio and establish a consistent method for documenting design work. The portfolio will demonstrate engineering analysis, decision-making and communication throughout the semester.

## Analyze

**Task A: Portfolio Analysis**

### Portfolio 1 - Thanh Tran - https://thanhvtran.com/

1- Navigability: The portfolio uses a simple navigation bar and a dedicated Projects page organized by category. A reader can locate a specific project in well under 60 seconds.

2- Reproducibility: The project pages describe the work performed and manufacturing processes used but generally do not provide enough dimensions, calculations or procedures for another engineer to fully reproduce the work.

3- Evidence of Reasoning: The portfolio includes evidence of engineering analysis such as torque calculations, press fits, testing and redesign for manufacturing. However, the criteria used to choose between design alternatives are not always explained.

4- Professional Tone: The project descriptions use concise engineering terminology and emphasize technical experience that would be relevant to an employer. Some humor is included on the homepage, making the presentation slightly less formal.

### Portfolio 2- Nathan Hoong - https://nhoong.github.io/

1- Navigability: Projects are separated by labeled headings with links to work experience, a resume and Github. A specific project can be located quickly, although the single-page layout requires more scrolling than a separate project index.

2- Reproducibility: Several projects include objectives, results, drawings and component descriptions. The linked capstone report provides additional calculations, requirements, testing procedures and drawings that would allow another engineer to understand much of the design process.

3- Evidence of Reasoning: The portfolio shows design objectives and results. While the capstone documentation provides stronger evidence through requirements, calculations, testing and validation.

4- Professional Tone: The language is primarily technical and project-focused, allowing an engineering employer to quickly identify the purpose and outcome of each project.
